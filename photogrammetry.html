Photogrammetry FAQ

What is a drone orthomosaic?+
An orthomosaic is a geometrically corrected, high-resolution aerial map stitched from hundreds of overlapping drone photos. Unlike a regular aerial photo, an orthomosaic has a consistent scale across the entire image and can be used for accurate measurements, import into CAD/GIS software, and time-lapse comparison.
What accuracy can I expect?+
Ground sample distance (GSD) of 1–2 cm/pixel is achievable at typical flight altitudes. With ground control points (GCPs), horizontal accuracy reaches 1–3 cm RMS. Without GCPs, expect 5–10 cm horizontal accuracy using the onboard RTK/PPK GNSS. We provide a full accuracy report with every deliverable.
How is photogrammetry different from LiDAR?+
Photogrammetry uses overlapping photographs and computer vision to reconstruct 3D geometry. It produces very high-resolution visual output (orthomosaics, textured 3D meshes) and is excellent in open areas. LiDAR uses laser pulses and is superior in vegetated areas where the laser can penetrate tree canopy to reach the bare earth. For construction sites, graded land, and hard surfaces, photogrammetry is often the better value. For forested terrain, topographic surveys, or flood plain modeling, LiDAR is preferred.
Can you repeat flights for construction progress tracking?+
Yes — and this is one of the most valuable applications. We fly consistent flight paths so each deliverable is directly comparable to previous flights. Progress reports are delivered showing exactly what changed between visits. Weekly, bi-weekly, and milestone-based schedules are all available.
